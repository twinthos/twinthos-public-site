Automation changes the shape of a job.

Historical analogies can start a discussion. They cannot forecast your staffing needs.

Twinthos · Published 21 October 2026 · Updated 8 November 2026 · 1 min read

THE SHORT ANSWER

In brief

Evaluate the tasks and service outcomes in your own business.

A story is not a forecast

The ATM is often used as an analogy for workplace automation. But an example from another industry cannot establish what will happen to your team. Demand, regulation, customer expectations and the remaining work all affect the outcome. We avoid treating the analogy as evidence of a guaranteed employment effect.

Look at the bundle of tasks

A role can include administration, judgement, relationship building and responsibility for unusual cases. Automating one part does not remove the rest. Map the work before making claims about an entire job becoming unnecessary.

Plan where the capacity goes

If a workflow frees time, decide how that time will be used. Faster customer responses, better quality checks and fewer missed follow-ups may be valuable outcomes. Measure them directly. Time returned is not automatically the same as money saved.

Keep people in the operating design

The people who do the work often know the exceptions that a process document misses. Include them in defining the job and reviewing early results. Twinthos’s purpose is to make people more capable; the deployment should be assessed against that purpose.

IN PRACTICE / ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO

An analogy cannot be your workforce forecast

Automation stories about bank machines are often used to imply a predictable employment outcome. That leap is not justified for a particular business. A role is a bundle of tasks, and the effect of automating one task depends on demand, process redesign, customer preferences and the remaining work. A receptionist, for example, might spend time on scheduling, unusual complaints, sensitive requests and maintaining relationships. Removing repetitive scheduling does not automatically remove the need for the other responsibilities.

THE OPERATING DECISION

Decide what people should do with the capacity

Ask frontline staff to describe the exceptions that are missing from the procedure. Automate a clearly bounded part of the role and measure service quality alongside time spent. Then decide what the business values more: faster responses, more thoughtful customer support, better quality assurance or a different allocation of attention. Those benefits need observation, not a historical anecdote. The useful question is whether the people and customers involved are better served by the redesigned work.

DIRECT ANSWER

Do ATMs prove that AI will increase or decrease jobs?

No. One technology's history does not establish another business's employment outcome. Evaluate the tasks, operating context and actual effects of a proposed deployment.

THE PRACTICAL TEST

Ask the people who perform the task what exceptions a process document misses, then include those cases in the trial.
