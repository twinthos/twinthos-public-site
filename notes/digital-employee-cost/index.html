Price the work, not the label.

A way to compare a managed AI service without inventing savings.

Twinthos · Published 21 October 2026 · Updated 8 November 2026 · 1 min read

THE SHORT ANSWER

In brief

Use your own baseline. Count review, exceptions and maintenance as part of the cost.

Measure the job today

Choose a representative period and record how often the task occurs, who handles it and what a correct result looks like. Include exceptions and rework. If the baseline is a guess, present the business case as a hypothesis to test rather than a promised return.

Define the service boundary

Ask whether the price includes setup, connectors, model usage, hosting, support and future changes. Agree a volume limit and what happens if it is exceeded. A small software subscription and a managed service cover different responsibilities, even if both use the term “AI employee”.

Compare like for like

Count the work people still do: approving sensitive actions, handling exceptions and checking results. A workflow may free capacity without reducing payroll. Separate time returned, cash savings and service improvements instead of collapsing them into one headline number.

IN PRACTICE / ILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO

Compare the same unit of work

If your team spends time preparing a recurring report, start by defining a finished report: required sources, format, review standard and delivery time. Record how many reports are produced, how long review takes and which failures require rework. When assessing an agent service, include the people who will verify its output. Saved preparation time is not necessarily cash saved, and new failure-handling work can offset some benefit. This gives a more credible baseline than comparing a service fee with the annual salary of a person whose role includes many unrelated responsibilities.

THE OPERATING DECISION

The quote is part of the design

Twinthos publishes a £999 one-off operations audit and a £3,500-per-month managed service for one agreed workflow, with a three-month minimum. The written agreement should make volume limits, extra connections, hosting, support arrangements and changes explicit. Those prices do not establish the result for any particular client. A useful decision is to begin with a measurable acceptance period and review the actual quality, time and exception load. Treat estimated savings as assumptions until the measured work supports them.

DIRECT ANSWER

How should I calculate AI automation return on investment?

Compare the fully scoped service cost with an observed baseline for the same task. Include human review, failures, tools, change management and the value of usable completed work.

THE PRACTICAL TEST

Use your own last month of work to record volume and review time. Compare it against the written scope, not an advertised percentage.
