The night the DWP page went quiet: 12 frames on 0 bytes and 8.4 million people

On Thursday evening people trying to check their benefits found a page that wouldn't open. Twelve frames on what the record shows, and the one question nobody has answered.

Tyson Mandela · Published 27 November 2026 · 3 min read

You're on a bus. You open the app to see whether this month's money has cleared. The page hangs, then hangs again. You assume your signal, then your password.

About a thousand people did the same in the next ninety minutes. At nine the department took services offline and called it a precaution. That word describes what you do, not what happened.

The one technical detail worth keeping: the secure connection still worked, and the system sent nothing back. Two other addresses had been deleted from the internet's directory. Somebody turned things off, one at a time.

So the question isn't whether there was a breach. It's who holds the list.

Every department now runs software that acts for it. Agents that read mail, look up cases, move files. Each one carries a key. When a system has to come down in an evening, you need a list of everything holding a key, who approved it, and when it expires.

Nobody has published one. I'd be surprised if one exists.

I build managed AI agents with a work log and one named approver. When a client's systems stop, the agents stop, and the log says what ran.
