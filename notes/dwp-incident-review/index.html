DWP cyber incident: 0 bytes, 8.4 million claimants, one missing list

At 19:20 on Thursday a thousand people tried to log into Universal Credit and couldn't. By Friday afternoon nobody had published a cause. These twelve frames are what I could see from outside: the public record, plus four tests run from a terminal at 16:10.

Tyson Mandela · Published 27 November 2026 · 4 min read

You're on a bus. You open the app to check whether this month's payment has cleared. The page hangs, then hangs again. You assume your signal, then your password, then you start to worry about the money.

About a thousand people did the same thing over the next ninety minutes. The department took some services offline at 21:00 and called it a precaution. That word describes what you do. It never describes what happened.

The question worth carrying into next week isn't "was there a breach". The honest answer is that nobody outside knows, and "no evidence" is a statement about evidence.

Ask who holds the list. Every department now runs software that acts on its behalf: agents that read mailboxes, query case systems, move files, call APIs. Each one carries credentials. When a system has to come down in an evening, the first thing you need is a list of everything holding a key to it, who approved it, and when it expires.

Nobody has published one for this incident. I'd be surprised if one exists.

I build managed AI agents with a company brain, a work log and one named approver. When a client's systems stop, the agents stop, and the log says what ran and who said yes.
