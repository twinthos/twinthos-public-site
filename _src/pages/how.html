How it works

Five steps from
your desk to done.

We learn the job, write down the rules, connect only the tools it needs and test the awkward cases. Then we run it. A named person at Twinthos is responsible for it.

The work happens
inside real systems.

The tools are configured for a particular role. Giving an agent a computer is not the same as granting unrestricted access.

01 / INSTRUCT

A job is assigned.

A person sends an agreed task through the configured channel.

02 / UNDERSTAND

Context is checked.

Approved information and known exceptions shape the response.

03 / OPERATE

Tools do the work.

Browser, files or connectors act only within the granted scope.

04 / VERIFY

A person stays accountable.

Review, activity evidence and escalation complete the workflow.

Five steps

  1. 01

    Understand

    We learn how the work is done today. What arrives, what should happen, and where it gets stuck.

  2. 02

    Define

    We agree what the software can do by itself and when it must ask someone.

  3. 03

    Connect

    We connect the tools you agreed to. Only those, and only for the agreed job.

  4. 04

    Test

    We try normal cases and unusual ones before anything goes live.

  5. 05

    Manage

    We check that it is working, fix problems and update it when your business changes.

The line

Routine tasks get done. Important decisions stay with you.

The line is written down before it goes live.

The digital employee may

  • Send approved messages and follow-ups
  • Record information in agreed systems
  • Ask for missing details
  • Assign and schedule the next step

A person always decides

  • Prices, discounts and refunds beyond agreed limits
  • Complaints and disputes
  • Anything outside the written rules
  • Changes to what's included, access or rules

Who does what

Your team

Gives the agreed access, confirms the rules, keeps them up to date and makes the decisions above the line.

Twinthos

Sets it up, tests it, keeps the tools connected and handles agreed problems.

Both of us

Agree what a good result looks like, then check what the software actually did.
